## The paradoxes {#paradoxes}

The research brings together contradictions described in different fields. Their resemblances are useful working comparisons, not one resolved structure for the performance.

<SourceLedger view="paradoxes" />


::: tip A recurring question
Several sources place being alone in relation to some form of presence, witness or response. Whether an audience does analogous work for Titania is a question for the studio, not an answer supplied by the books.

Bergson complicates the comparison: shared laughter and sequence matter differently from the reliable presence described by Winnicott or the inner dialogue described by Arendt.
:::

## A working proposition {#theorem}

Read beside one another, the sources suggest a proposition worth testing:

> An act done alone carries no meaning until it is witnessed, and its meaning is fixed by who witnesses it rather than by what is done.

<SourceLedger view="statements" />


This is the project's synthesis, not a conclusion shared by the sources. It can sharpen observation of audience presence, attention, failure and wordlessness. It does not prescribe a scene, sequence or outcome.

## A recurring pattern {#involution}

Several entries describe a condition being shaped by something that appears to undo it:

- **Winnicott** — the capacity to be alone is built by not having been alone.
- **Arendt** — solitude is being two, so the self is company until company arrives and makes it one.
- **Gaulier**, in Purcell Gates’ account — laughter is earned by failing to earn it.
- **Bergson** — laughter needs an echo, so a room laughing at solitude disproves the solitude it is laughing at.
- **Montaigne**, reversed — the room kept wholly to oneself stays furnished with other people’s words.

No two fields name the condition in the same way: Winnicott’s *being alone*, Arendt’s *solitude*, Montaigne’s *back shop*. The ledger keeps each author's own term rather than flattening the distinctions.

The resemblance may help the studio notice a contradiction. Rehearsal may also contradict it or make it irrelevant.

## The open question {#open}

::: warning An uncited distinction
Letting the self loosen rather than consoling it may point to Weil, to a reading of non-self, or to contemplative solitude. No source in the current ledger establishes that connection, so it remains a research question rather than a basis for material.
:::

The ledger records the auguste as the project's clown lineage. That lineage may sharpen how play, failure and audience contact are observed without supplying material in advance.

## What this ledger does not cover {#boundary}

Two limits, stated so that neither has to be discovered by a reader.

**The reading is European.** Twenty-one works, all of them French, German, English or Swiss in origin. There is no non-Western clown or comic tradition here, no trickster scholarship, no Bakhtin. The project works inside a European physical-comedy lineage and claims nothing beyond it; where the pages say *solitude*, they mean the condition as that lineage describes it, not a universal.

**A claim without a source is the project's own.** The ledger gives the provenance of every borrowed idea. Studio observations and working propositions remain practitioner knowledge unless a source supports them; a source does not turn them automatically into artistic decisions.

## A correction worth keeping {#correction}

::: danger There is no book called *In Praise of Flop*
It is a plausible-sounding title that does not exist. The citable scholarship on Gaulier’s flop is Lucy Amsden’s 2017 article in *Theatre, Dance and Performance Training* and her 2015 Glasgow doctoral thesis. Use those in any bibliography that goes to a funder.
:::
