# Sources & lineages

<MediaFigure id="a-0679147602f4d57d" />

This page records research gathered around **Solo Titania Chaos**.

It is not a reading diary and not a theoretical blueprint for the performance.

The sources help distinguish different forms of being alone, question assumptions about clown, audience and failure, and trace some of the artistic lineages behind the work.

Some ideas come directly from sources. Others are connections made by the project. The two are kept separate.

**Research sharpens observation. The studio decides what becomes playable.**

## A map of the research {#a-map-of-the-research}

### Being alone

The research distinguishes **solitude, loneliness and isolation** rather than treating them as different degrees of the same state.

Being alone also does not always mean that others are simply absent. The research looks at inner dialogue, undemanding presence, and the traces other people leave in objects, language and habit.

**Key references:** Hannah Arendt, D. W. Winnicott, Michel de Montaigne, Sherry Turkle.

### Clown and audience

A clown is never entirely alone once somebody is watching.

Complicité, attention, laughter and response make the audience part of the event even when nobody speaks or comes on stage.

This creates a formal difficulty for a clown piece about solitude: the art form depends on relationship while the subject investigates being alone.

**Key references:** Philippe Gaulier, Henri Bergson, Jacques Lecoq, Vincent Laughery.

### Failure and authenticity

Clown practice makes failure unusually visible.

The research distinguishes between prepared failure, genuine failure in the room, failure to amuse, and the moment when the clown registers what has happened.

It also asks whether a real failure necessarily makes a performance more authentic — or whether a sense of authenticity can itself be created on stage.

**Key references:** Lucy Amsden, Laura Purcell Gates, Jon Davison, Jacques Lecoq, Philippe Gaulier.

### Lineage and physical form

The project sits within a European lineage of physical theatre and clown rather than within a literary model of theatre.

Relevant references include movement observation, the search for one's own clown, the **Auguste tradition**, economy and stillness, and direct relation with the audience.

These lineages offer tools and histories. They do not determine the form of the solo.

**Key references:** Jacques Lecoq, Étienne Decroux, Pascal Jacob, Grock, Klara Van Wyk, Jon Davison.

## Questions and contradictions {#questions-and-contradictions}

These are not conclusions of the performance. They are questions worth carrying into the studio.

### Solitude can contain company

Arendt describes solitude through the idea of being **two-in-one**: being with oneself rather than simply being without others.

Winnicott approaches aloneness differently, but also places it in relation to another presence.

**Question for the work:** What changes in Titania's behaviour under different conditions of being alone, connected or disconnected?

### Being alone may depend on another person

Winnicott's account of the capacity to be alone is founded on the paradox of being alone in the presence of someone who makes no immediate demand.

**Question for the work:** What changes when Titania plays alone while an audience is present?

### Laughter makes company audible

Bergson treats laughter as social: it reverberates within a group.

For this project, that suggests a contradiction:

> **A room laughing at solitude is also a room making its own togetherness audible.**

This is a **project inference drawing on Bergson**, not a statement by Bergson.

**Question for the work:** What does laughter do to Titania's state of being alone on stage?

### Comedy and vulnerability may pull in different directions

Bergson also argues that emotion can interfere with laughter.

That complicates a piece that wants genuine laughter without excluding vulnerability, intimacy or melancholy.

**Question for the work:** What happens to the comedy when a moment becomes vulnerable — and what happens to the vulnerability when the audience laughs?

### A clown needs the audience she cannot control

Clown practice depends on response, complicité and the possibility of failure in front of others.

> **The clown cannot be alone while the audience is present, and cannot perform without them.**

The studio does not need to solve this contradiction in advance.

It can test what it makes Titania do.

## The source ledger {#the-source-ledger}

The ledger records where ideas come from and where the project's own interpretation begins.

### Status

**Verified**

The citation and the claim attributed to the source have been checked against the source. This does **not** mean that the performer personally read the work in full.

**Probable**

The connection appears relevant but has not been established strongly enough to treat it as verified.

**Chosen lineage**

A source or tradition is being used consciously as part of the artistic lineage of the project. This does not mean that it determines the final form.

**Project inference**

The connection or conclusion belongs to the project rather than to the cited author.

### Hannah Arendt — solitude, loneliness and isolation

**Status:** Verified

**Source finding:** Arendt distinguishes isolation, loneliness and solitude as different conditions rather than degrees of one condition.

**For this project:** The distinction prevents physical aloneness from automatically being treated as loneliness.

**Studio question:** What changes in Titania's behaviour under different conditions of being alone, connected or disconnected?

**Reference:** Arendt, H. (1953). Ideology and terror: A novel form of government. *The Review of Politics, 15*(3), 303–327. https://doi.org/10.1017/S0034670500001510

### Hannah Arendt — the two-in-one

**Status:** Verified

**Source finding:** Arendt describes solitude as an inner dialogue: being with oneself rather than simply being without others.

**For this project:** This offers one way of thinking about aloneness that is not simply absence.

**Studio question:** What happens physically when Titania has no immediate relation except to herself?

**Reference:** Arendt, H. (1978). *The life of the mind*. Harcourt Brace Jovanovich.

### D. W. Winnicott — the capacity to be alone

**Status:** Verified

**Source finding:** Winnicott describes the capacity to be alone through the paradox of being alone in the presence of a reliable, non-demanding other.

**For this project:** Presence is worth testing rather than automatically treating it as the opposite of solitude. This does not establish that an audience performs Winnicott's function.

**Studio question:** How does Titania's playing change when somebody is there but asks nothing of her?

**Reference:** Winnicott, D. W. (1958). The capacity to be alone. *The International Journal of Psychoanalysis, 39*, 416–420.

### Michel de Montaigne — the back shop

**Status:** Verified

**Source finding:** Montaigne proposes the *arrière-boutique*, a private inner space kept wholly for oneself.

**Project inference:** A private space may still contain habits, memories, borrowed language and traces of other people. That reversal belongs to the project, not to Montaigne.

**Studio question:** What remains physically present when another person is absent?

**Reference:** Montaigne, M. de. (1958). Of solitude. In D. M. Frame (Trans.), *The complete works of Montaigne: Essays, travel journal, letters*. Stanford University Press. (Original work published 1580)

### Henri Bergson — laughter and the group

**Status:** Verified

**Source finding:** Bergson describes laughter as social and in need of an echo within a group.

**Project inference:** A laughing audience makes its own togetherness audible.

**Studio question:** What does that shared response change in Titania's playing?

**Reference:** Bergson, H. (1900). *Le rire: Essai sur la signification du comique*. Félix Alcan.

### Henri Bergson — laughter and emotion

**Status:** Verified

**Source finding:** Bergson argues that emotional identification can interfere with comic response.

**For this project:** This complicates any simple assumption that laughter and emotional exposure work in the same way at the same moment.

**Studio question:** What happens to the comedy when a moment becomes vulnerable, and what happens to the vulnerability when the audience laughs?

**Reference:** Bergson, H. (1900). *Le rire: Essai sur la signification du comique*. Félix Alcan.

### Henri Bergson — comic unconsciousness

**Status:** Verified

**Source finding:** Bergson connects comic effect with a character's limited awareness of what others can see.

**For this project:** This can be compared with clown traditions in which noticing failure becomes part of the comic event. The two positions do not need to be reconciled in theory.

**Studio question:** When does awareness strengthen a comic moment, and when does it end it?

**Reference:** Bergson, H. (1900). *Le rire: Essai sur la signification du comique*. Félix Alcan.

### Jacques Lecoq — movement and the search for one's own clown

**Status:** Chosen lineage

**Source finding:** Lecoq connects movement observation, audience effect and the search for one's own clown. His published writing on clown is relatively limited.

**For this project:** Observable effect matters more than theoretical intention. This lineage does not prescribe a structure for the solo.

**Studio question:** What does Titania actually make happen in the room?

**Reference:** Lecoq, J. (2000). *The moving body: Teaching creative theatre* (D. Bradby, Trans.). Methuen. https://doi.org/10.5040/9781350053496

### Philippe Gaulier — complicité

**Status:** Chosen lineage

**Source finding:** Gaulier uses *complicité* for a spontaneous, often unspoken understanding between performers and with an audience.

**For this project:** The concept matters because Titania's relation with an audience does not depend on verbal exchange. It does not prove that the solo must be wordless.

**Reference:** Gaulier, P. (2007). *Mes pensées sur le théâtre: Ex Le gégèneur = My thoughts on theatre: Ex The tormentor* (E. Maclachlan, Trans.). Éditions Filmiko.

### Lucy Amsden — the flop

**Status:** Verified

**Source finding:** Amsden examines different registers of failure in Gaulier's clown training, including prepared failure, genuine failure and failure to amuse.

**For this project:** Failure can be observed without ranking it numerically.

**Studio question:** What kind of failure occurred, what became exposed, and what followed?

**Reference:** Amsden, L. (2017). Monsieur Marcel and Monsieur Flop: Failure in clown training at Ecole Philippe Gaulier. *Theatre, Dance and Performance Training, 8*(2), 129–142. https://doi.org/10.1080/19443927.2017.1316304

### Laura Purcell Gates — failure and authenticity

**Status:** Verified

**Source finding:** Purcell Gates examines narratives of authenticity and the difference between genuine and rehearsed failure in French clown training.

**For this project:** Failure need not be treated as automatic proof of authenticity.

**Studio question:** What matters in the room: how the failure originated, or what Titania does with it?

**Reference:** Purcell Gates, L. (2011). Locating the self: Narratives and practices of authenticity in French clown training. *Theatre, Dance and Performance Training, 2*(2), 231–242. https://doi.org/10.1080/19443927.2011.553239

### Jon Davison — authenticity as an effect

**Status:** Verified

**Source finding:** Davison challenges the assumption that clown failure simply reveals an authentic self beneath performance and treats authenticity as a theatrical effect.

**For this project:** This is an important objection to treating “real” failure as automatically superior to performed failure.

**Studio question:** Does the distinction remain useful once material meets an audience?

**Reference:** Davison, J. (2013). *Clown: Readings in theatre practice*. Palgrave Macmillan.

### Étienne Decroux — economy and stillness

**Status:** Chosen lineage

**Source finding:** Decroux's writing attends to bodily movement, pause, weight, resistance and stillness.

**For this project:** This supports attention to what the body can carry without explanatory language. It does not determine the movement vocabulary of the solo.

**Reference:** Decroux, É. (1985). *Words on mime* (2nd ed.; M. Piper, Trans.). Pomona College Theatre Department.

### Vincent Laughery — the fall and the audience

**Status:** Verified

**Source finding:** Laughery considers the clown's fall and listening as ways of renewing relation and reaching common ground with an audience.

**For this project:** Failure may be understood as relation rather than simply defeat.

**Reference:** Laughery, V. (2022). L'écoute et la chute: Le sens du lieu commun dans le jeu clownesque et ses échos dans l'écriture poétique. *Fabula / Les colloques*. https://doi.org/10.58282/colloques.8603

### Sherry Turkle — constant connection and experienced isolation

**Status:** Verified

**Source finding:** Turkle studies how constant technological connection can coexist with experienced isolation.

**For this project:** This is a contemporary reference point, not a requirement that technology or digital media appear in the performance.

**Reference:** Turkle, S. (2011). *Alone together: Why we expect more from technology and less from each other*. Basic Books.

### Pascal Jacob — the Auguste

**Status:** Chosen lineage

**Source finding:** Jacob traces the Auguste as a named clown type and tradition.

**For this project:** The Auguste is a useful lineage through which to consider aspects of Titania's clown practice. It does not establish a Whiteface–Auguste structure for the solo.

**Reference:** Jacob, P. (n.d.). *L'Auguste*. Bibliothèque nationale de France & Centre national des arts du cirque. https://cirque-cnac.bnf.fr/fr/clowns/profils-du-clown/auguste

### Grock — the solo Auguste

**Status:** Chosen lineage

**Source finding:** Grock provides a historical precedent for an Auguste working beyond the conventional Whiteface pairing and carrying clown performance into the theatre.

**For this project:** The precedent is historical, not instructional.

**Reference:** Grock. (1931). *Life's a lark* (E. Behrens, Ed.; M. Pemberton, Trans.). William Heinemann.

### Klara Van Wyk — Whiteface and Auguste

**Status:** Verified

**Source finding:** Van Wyk examines structure and spontaneity through the functions of Whiteface and Auguste in contemporary clown theatre.

**For this project:** Her work makes the relationship between structure and spontaneity worth noticing. It does **not** establish that the audience, room or an object must function as Titania's Whiteface.

**Reference:** Van Wyk, K. (2015). *The Whiteface and the Auguste: The integration of structure and spontaneity in contemporary clown theatre performance* [Master's dissertation, University of Cape Town]. OpenUCT. http://hdl.handle.net/11427/20128

## Limits and corrections {#limits-and-corrections}

### The research has a limited cultural frame

The current research is concentrated in European and North American philosophy, psychology, physical theatre and clown traditions.

It does not constitute a universal account of solitude or comedy.

There is currently no substantial research here into non-Western clown traditions, trickster traditions or other cultural models of being alone.

The scope should therefore remain explicit rather than universalising its conclusions.

### A sourced idea and a project idea are not the same thing

**A claim without a source is the project's own.**

Even when a source is relevant, the connection made by Solo Titania Chaos may still be an inference, hypothesis or studio question.

Citation does not turn that connection into an artistic fact.

### Verified does not mean personally read in full

The research around the project has been gathered through different forms of source work.

**Verified** means that a citation and the claim attributed to it have been checked against the source.

It does not indicate that Titania personally read every work listed here from beginning to end.

### A correction worth keeping

There is no Philippe Gaulier book called *In Praise of Flop*. Earlier research contained this plausible but incorrect title.

Provenance also means keeping a visible record when a research error is corrected.
