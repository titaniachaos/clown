# The work

Three faces of one argument: what the solo is about, how it is shaped, and who completes it. Everything here is a rehearsal hypothesis until a room answers it.

## Artistic concept {#concept}

> Solitude is not the absence of relationship. It is a relationship with an absence.

The solo treats solitude as a condition with many temperatures: chosen and imposed solitude, loneliness, isolation, private consciousness, sacred retreat, absence and the solitude of the final exit. Each mode carries its own comic possibility and its own reversal.

### Nine modes of solitude {#modes}

#### 1. The given

Every person occupies one body and one private consciousness. The comedy begins when that body behaves like an inconvenient roommate: it itches, tires, interrupts and refuses transcendence.

#### 2. Solitude, loneliness and isolation

Solitude can contain an inner dialogue; loneliness is the collapse of that company; isolation is separation from acting with others. The stage can move among all three without treating them as synonyms.

#### 3. The chosen room

Retreat can be pleasurable and necessary. Its reversal is that the private room remains furnished with borrowed language, remembered gestures and absent people.

#### 4. The imposed cell

When separation is forced, the mind manufactures company from walls, marks, objects and rituals. The comic act of invention can reveal the violence of the condition without illustrating it solemnly.

#### 5. Absence as relationship

An empty chair is not neutral when it holds the shape of someone missing. Solitude proves a capacity for relationship: only a creature capable of company can experience its absence.

#### 6. Learning to be alone

The capacity to play alone develops in safe company. This is the project’s theatrical hinge because an audience provides exactly that presence for a solo performer.

#### 7. Alone together

Noise, feeds and frictionless connection can protect us from silence while leaving us untouched. The same condition may be comic relief, defence and genuine loneliness.

#### 8. The emptied room

Chosen solitude may become a practice of letting the self loosen rather than a project of consoling it. Even here, a fly can interrupt enlightenment.

#### 9. The final exit

Some thresholds cannot be crossed by another person. Yet vigils, farewells and audiences form communities around that irreducible passage.

### The central question

Can a wordless clown solo make an audience experience solitude rather than watch it depicted?

The clown is the right instrument because clowning is built on complicity. A clown comes alive through the audience’s attention, shared breath and laughter. Performing solitude therefore creates a live paradox: the clown cannot be alone while the audience is present, but cannot perform without them.

### The smallest dramatic unit

The **flop** is a brief experience of exposure. Something fails, the clown is caught without protection, and the audience’s laughter restores contact. Repeating that cycle at different intensities allows comedy and vulnerability to share the same structure.

### Wordlessness as material

Silence is not a limitation to overcome. Language is one of our technologies of company, so a performance without words begins inside the condition it investigates. Breath, movement, objects, light and the audience’s response become the vocabulary.

### Artistic lineages

The research draws on physical movement analysis, economy and stillness, direct audience contact, playful anarchy, and clown material grounded in observed human behaviour. Every image must pass a practical test: has a real person done something like this when they believed nobody was watching?

The [source ledger](/sources#ledger) traces these lineages back to the books they come from, and records playful anarchy as settled: the auguste.

## Seven-movement dramaturgy {#dramaturgy}

The current 45-to-60-minute arc moves from **alone** to **accompanied**, through pleasure, exposure, confinement and doubling, toward release. The structure remains a rehearsal hypothesis: timing and order must earn their place in front of an audience.

### 1. The empty space

A body arrives and discovers its weight, edges and inconvenient needs. No object and no acknowledged audience—yet.

### 2. The discovery of the other

The clown sees the audience. Terror turns into delight, attention becomes necessary, and complicity begins.

### 3. The back shop

A private room and its small rituals are assembled. Solitude becomes pleasurable, precise and domestic, sustained by the quiet presence of witnesses.

### 4. The flop

The room betrays its maker. A staircase of failures exposes how laughter can rescue the clown—and what happens when one failure receives silence.

### 5. The cell

The same room becomes a prison when a rule appears. Repetition, resistant space and attempted escape turn isolation into physical action.

### 6. The two-in-one

An ordinary object becomes company. A complete relationship appears inside one performer: meeting, play, conflict, separation and the choice to let go.

### 7. The exit

The room is dismantled object by object. The performance returns to the opening emptiness, now charged by everything that has passed through it.

### What Arendt's structure requires {#arendt}

Read in the source, the three terms are not degrees of one condition. They are three different situations, and two movements depend on the difference.

**Isolation is productive.** Arendt insists it is required for all making: the maker withdraws from common concerns and, in isolation, stays in contact with the world as human artifice. Movement 3 is therefore not a milder cell. It is fabrication, and its rituals are work.

**The cell is made by stopping the making, not by adding a wall.** Isolation becomes unbearable, and turns into loneliness, only when the capacity to add something of one's own to the common world is destroyed. The rule that appears in movement 5 should prevent the room from being added to. A restriction that merely confines the body leaves the maker intact, and the movement will not turn.

**The two-in-one is not self-sufficient, and this is the hinge of the hour.** In solitude the self is two. Arendt's difficulty is that the two-in-one needs other people in order to become one again: identity is confirmed only from outside, and company restores the single voice. Solitude splits. Only being seen makes whole.

So the audience is not a witness to the clown's solitude. It is the thing that ends it. Movement 6 builds a companion that cannot perform that office — an object can hold presence, as Winnicott allows, but it cannot confirm an identity, which Arendt requires — and that is why it must be let go while the room is still full.

**Solitude can become loneliness** when one is deserted by one's own self. That is the risk the middle of the hour runs, and the reason the flops must keep landing: laughter is the evidence that the others are still there.

## Audience relationship {#audience}

The audience is not a passive witness. Its presence makes the solo possible and simultaneously disproves the clown’s claim to be alone.

### The first discovery

The clown begins without acknowledging the room. Seeing the audience must therefore register as an event rather than a convention. The first look establishes curiosity, danger, delight and need.

### Complicity

The performer tests whether the audience remains available: a glance, a shared breath, a small failure, a return after leaving. Contact should become dependable enough that its later withdrawal has a cost.

### Present without making demands

Winnicott's condition is narrower than "company", and the narrowness is the point. What builds the capacity to be alone is a presence that is reliable and makes no claim: someone available, attending, asking for nothing. An audience that demands is not that presence. Neither is one that has stopped attending.

He also allows that the reliable other need not be a person in the ordinary sense. It may be represented by a cot, a pram, or the general atmosphere of the room. The invented companion is therefore not a substitute for presence but a carrier of it, which is why it can work completely before it is lost.

What the condition permits is floundering: existing for a time without reacting to anything and without a direction of interest, until an impulse arrives that feels real rather than manufactured. That is the state this solo needs, and the hardest one to hold in front of people.

The failure mode is named too. Its opposite, in Winnicott's phrase, is a false life built on reactions to external stimuli — the performer who only answers the room. A purely reactive clown is never alone on stage, and the theme cannot appear at all.

The practical test is therefore not whether the audience is warm. It is whether the clown can stop reacting to them and still be watched.

### Laughter as rescue

After a flop, laughter restores relationship. The show can repeat this small abandonment-and-rescue cycle at increasing levels of exposure. One deliberately unrescued failure then becomes structurally different from ordinary silence.

### Being replaced

When the invented companion comes alive, the clown can forget the audience. For a short period the room experiences what it means not to be needed. The theme is no longer explained; it is distributed across the spectators’ bodies.

### Withdrawal

The late movement can remove company in layers:

1. Familiar sound disappears.
2. Light no longer finds the clown.
3. Material that previously produced contact no longer receives it.

These layers must be tested separately. Audience non-response cannot be reliably rehearsed in private.

### The ending contract

If the audience leaves while the clown remains, front-of-house, light and timing become part of the dramaturgy. The exit must be designed with the venue rather than treated as an acting choice alone.
