# Every way this workspace could file itself

The archive next door is bound like Queneau's sonnets: nineteen positions,
dozens of photographs able to fill each, and turning one line leaves the other
eighteen where they were. That works because the positions are independent —
using a photograph in one place does not stop it being used in another.

Here they are not, and that is the whole interest of it. This workspace holds
eleven pieces of writing and asks eleven questions, and every piece carries the
questions it is actually about. A filing gives each question one piece and uses
every piece exactly once: a complete account, with nothing said twice and
nothing left out. There are 1482.

## Why turning one line moves another

Take the piece answering *solitude* and give it to *audience*, and something
else must come to *solitude* — every piece is still used exactly once. In
combinatorics a filing is a system of distinct representatives; the constraint
is what makes it an account rather than a shuffle.

That is also why the address is a plain number rather than the archive's
mixed-radix one. There, most numbers are arrangements and the few that are not
can be refused. Here the same scheme would give 3,810,240 numbers of which 1482
are filings — one in every 2,570 — and an address that usually lands somewhere
else is not an address. So the filings are enumerated in a fixed order and
numbered 0 to 1481, and every number is one.

## What it will not arrange

The ledger. Every record there attaches a phrase in this documentation to the
work it draws on, with a status saying whether that work was read at first
hand. A machine that paired those differently would be inventing scholarship,
and the receipts exist precisely to make that impossible. This arranges the
writing, which is about topics it genuinely carries, and leaves the citations
where they were put.
